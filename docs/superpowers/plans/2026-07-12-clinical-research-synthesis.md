## Cross-cutting themes

- **NilaMind is structurally unguided, and unguided digital interventions consistently underperform guided ones.** Four+ domains independently cite this ceiling — self-help ACT (French et al. 2017), iCBT for GAD (Cuijpers et al. 2010; Richards & Richardson 2012), guided vs. unguided iCBT for depression (Karyotaki et al. 2017), unguided OCD/exposure self-help (Imai et al. 2022). No upgrade can fully solve this; the honest response (already the pattern across corrected upgrades) is calibrated, non-overclaiming copy, not a promise of guided-level effect sizes.

- **Measurement/monitoring alone doesn't help — it needs to close into a feedback loop.** ema-checkins (mood monitoring alone shows no robust symptom effect, per Astill Wright et al. 2026), dbt-skills (diary card has no feedback loop; skills-use, not tracking, is the mediator, per Neacsiu et al. 2010), ba-pst (moodBefore is dead code, breaking the before/after learning signal), and measurement (MBC's evidence is for clinician-driven feedback, which NilaMind structurally lacks) all land on the same fix: data being *written* but never *read back* to the user in an actionable form.

- **One-pass/single-session protocols contradict an evidence base that is uniformly multi-week.** ba-pst's BA protocol self-erases on completion; dbt-skills' 8-step tour fires once; the effective digital analogs in the literature (Alber et al. 2023, Wilks et al. 2018) all ran multiple weeks with repeated practice. This is flagged as a gap in at least 3 domains independently.

- **If-then implementation intentions are a recurring, cheap, high-leverage fix wherever the app asks someone to plan an action.** ba-pst/PST action plans, and the engagement-onboarding daily-intention redesign, both independently converge on the same citation (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, d=0.65 / d=0.61 for overcoming failure-to-start) as the single best-evidenced, lowest-effort change available.

- **Specific/interactive/structured framing consistently beats vague/passive framing of the same technique.** dbt-skills (perspective-taking reappraisal > passive Check-the-Facts card), gad-worry (metacognitive-experiment framing of postponement > pure stimulus control), engagement-onboarding (if-then plan > open "what's your intention" question) — the same underlying mechanism (specificity forces engagement) shows up across unrelated clinical domains.

- **Deterministic, on-device safety-state gating (crisis scan, elevation guard, mood-state) exists for chat but isn't consistently extended to other tools carrying comparable risk.** act-exposure flags this for Exposure Hierarchy and Values-to-Action (no elevation-guard consultation before committing to an ambitious action or fear ladder); ema-checkins flags it for EMA cadence (no throttling when a deterioration trend is already flagged); crisis-safety flags it as a detection-layer/surface-intensity mismatch. All three want the app to apply a pattern it has already built once, elsewhere.

- **Streak/gamification design is actively de-recommended, not just unexamined.** ema-checkins and engagement-onboarding both independently cite Six et al. (2021) (gamification: no depression-outcome or adherence benefit) to argue for demoting/not-adding streak-style surfaces; engagement-onboarding adds Mildner et al. (2024) on dark-pattern harms. This is one of the few places the research explicitly argues against a category of feature outright, not just for incremental improvement.

- **Internal "basis strings"/citations that justify existing features frequently cite the wrong effect size — usually a full multi-component/guided-package number attached to an isolated or unguided technique.** crisis-safety (SPI ideation claim), ba-pst ("SMD≈-0.51" for BA), gad-worry ("d≈-0.9" for worry-postponement alone) all made the identical category of error: borrowing the headline number from a bigger, better-supported intervention package to justify a smaller piece of it. This is worth a standing engineering review pass across all `basis`/evidence-comment strings in the codebase, not just the four caught here.

- **The adversarial verification pass consistently found real, on-topic papers attached to a wrong specific number or wrong author — never a fully fabricated citation.** This pattern repeated in nearly every domain (crisis-safety, alliance-voice, ba-pst, measurement, rhythm-sleep, act-exposure, grounding-peer, engagement-onboarding all had at least one "WRONG-CLAIM, not fabrication" finding). The fix in every case was correcting the citation, not deleting the underlying claim or upgrade — a genuinely reassuring signal about the underlying research quality, but also a standing argument for verifying exact numbers/authors before any citation goes into user-facing copy or code comments.

- **Brief/single-session interventions and short sessions already capture most of the achievable benefit — the evidence argues against "more is better" by default.** grounding-peer (a single 5-minute breathing session matches longer sessions on vagal-activity gains), gad-worry (a single-session IU intervention produced large effects), and measurement (retesting PHQ-9/GAD-7 faster than weekly mostly re-rates the same 2-week recall window) all independently support capping dose/frequency rather than maximizing it.
